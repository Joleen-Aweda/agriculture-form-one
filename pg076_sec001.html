Safety precautions in the use of pesticides:

Pesticides are toxic chemicals and should be handled with care. Therefore, when using pesticides, you should take the following precautions:

  • (a)Always check the date they expire and read the instructions carefully.
  • (b)Wear protective gear, such as masks, gloves and field boots.
  • (c)Avoid breathing in the pesticide.
  • (d)Don’t let the pesticide touch your food or water.
  • (e)Wash your hands and clothes after handling pesticides.
  • (f)Don’t smoke or eat while using pesticides.
  • (g)Clean the spraying equipment after using it.
  • (h)Store the chemicals in a safe place, out of reach of children and animals.
  • (i)Don’t throw leftover containers in gardens.
  • (j)Always wait for the recommended time before harvesting a treated crop, that is, adhere to the withdrawal period.
  • (k)Keep a record of dates and all the chemicals you use. This helps you to determine when to harvest your crops.

It is important to note that while chemical pest control is faster and more predictable than other methods, it can be expensive and harmful to humans, animals, and the environment. Therefore, it is important to use these chemicals safely and responsibly, considering their effects on the environment and other beneficial organisms.

Integrated pest management

Integrated Pest Management (IPM) is a way to control pests using a combination of various pest control methods such as cultural, biological, physical, and chemical controls. These methods are chosen based on information about pests and their interactions with the environment. The following are some steps to follow when using IPM:

  • (i)Choose pest control methods that are safe for crops, soil, air, water and people.
  • (ii)Use differences between crops and pests to give crops a competitive advantage over pests.
