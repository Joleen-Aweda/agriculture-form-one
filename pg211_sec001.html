Feeding of indigenous chickens

These chickens need to search for their food and water. In this way, chickens can meet some of their daily needs. However, they can only meet one-third of their daily requirements. As a result, they grow slowly and produce less meat and eggs. Supplementing with a balanced ration helps them to grow faster and produce more. Indigenous chickens also require clean water all day for their health.

Chicken farmers should give adequate feed to the birds to make them healthier. The indigenous chickens need to have adequate feed every day. One can plan better feeding programmes. Use the available resources on the farm to make simple rations for the birds. If pulse and cereal grains are grown together on the farms, one can make extra rations for the birds. For example, sorghum, maize, and millet can provide energy, while pigeon peas and cowpeas can provide protein for the birds. In areas where paddy or maize is grown, by-products such as brans for the bird’s rations can be used. Another way to give extra feed, especially protein to the chickens, is by growing earthworms, edible larvae, and insects such as ants and cockroaches. You can feed these to the chickens together with cereal grains or cereal by-products. Indigenous chickens can also be given good food leftovers from the farmers’ meals.

Parasite and disease management in indigenous chickens

Indigenous chickens are tough and can resist many parasites and diseases. However, if not cared for, they can still fall sick, especially when searching for food. For example, Newcastle disease can kill more than 60% of chickens in the dry seasons. Parasites do not usually kill the chickens, but they can lower their productivity.
