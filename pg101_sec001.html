When choosing which onion to grow, think about these factors:

(a)

Disease resistance: Some onion varieties are more resistant to diseases than others. It is advisable to choose a disease-resistant variety to ensure a healthy crop.

(b)

Market demand: Consider the preferences of your potential customers. In Tanzania, onions are commonly used in various dishes, so varieties that are flavourful and have a longer shelf life are often more popular.

(c)

Growth duration: The time taken for an onion variety to mature can influence your choice. If you want a quicker harvest, choose a variety that matures early. Always find out more and ask local crop extension workers or experienced onion farmers for advice. This will help you decide on the best onion variety to grow.

Nursery preparation

Onion seeds are usually not sown directly in the field. We use nursery beds, which can be raised, sunken or flat. These beds should be levelled to prevent water from gathering in one place. If water gathers and stays, it can create a good environment for the development of fungal diseases. The amount of seed used is between 5 - 7 kg per hectare. This depends on the space between plants and the variety of onions
