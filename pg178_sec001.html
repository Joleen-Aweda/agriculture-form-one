Management of temperature and comfort for chicks

It is necessary to heat the brooder for the required period. The actual length of time depends on the outside weather conditions as well as the rate of feathering of the birds. Heating of the brooder should be started 12 - 48 hours before the arrival of chicks. During this time, heater should be regulated to achieve a temperature of about 35°C when measured 5 cm above the litter at the edge or at the border of the brooder. This temperature is adequate for the chicks on the first day.

If possible, thermometers should be used before chicks are placed in the brooder. After the first two days, thermometers may be removed. As chicks grow older, the temperature is reduced at the rates you have already learnt. One should be guided by the chick’s behaviour you have learnt in the previous Chapter (refer to Figures 9.11 (a) to (d)). These will help to regulate the heat supply or take any corrective measures. When the chicks are fully feathered, it is no longer necessary to provide heat. Brooder heat may be increased beyond the guidelines if chicks appear chilled especially during stress created by vaccination or beak trimming. This will help to keep birds comfortable at all times.

Management of ventilation for chicks

Ventilation means the circulation of fresh air through the enclosed space. Good ventilation is important for the provision of oxygen and for removing ammonia gas, carbon dioxide gas and dust from the brooder. It also eliminates the bacteria of air-borne diseases. The movement of air in the brooding house must be controlled in such a way that even temperature and fresh air are maintained. This helps to meet the best conditions required by the young chicks. Chicks are susceptible to the cold and extreme wind (draughts) and therefore must be protected. To achieve this, curtains must be hung down the length of the walls. The size of the opening allowed to let fresh air in the brooder house without chilling the birds will depend on the weather. As the chicks grow older, the curtains may be opened more during the warm hours of the day and kept closed during the hot hours of the day. The common curtains used are those made up of plastic-like materials.

Management of lighting for layer chicks

Lighting is one of the important aspects that influences successful egg production. It should be considered as a continuous process from the day old to the end of the laying cycle. The programme used in lighting should be related to the growing period. This is because day length directly influences sexual maturity hence influencing egg size, particularly in early production. During the brooding period, provide 23 hours of light for the first 2 - 3 days then provide 22 hours of light up to the third week.
