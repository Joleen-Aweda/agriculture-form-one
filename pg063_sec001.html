Management of soil fertility for vegetable production

Soil is a natural medium where plants grow and get their nutrients. However, sometimes soil becomes unhealthy or infertile. The following subsections will discuss the causes of loss of soil fertility and the use of organic and inorganic fertilisers in maintaining soil fertility.

Causes of loss of soil fertility

There are several causes of soil fertility lose or depletion, some of which are discussed here.

Soil erosion:

Soil erosion happens when the moving wind or water removes the topsoil from the land. This can happen when there is a lot of rainfall or when the land is not covered with vegetation. When soil erosion happens, the nutrients in the soil can be washed away, making it infertile. To combat this, we can plant trees and other vegetations to protect the soil from being washed or blown away. We can also use contour farming in sloppy areas to minimise soil erosion (refer to Figure 4.4).
