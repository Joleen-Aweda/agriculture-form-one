Chicks may be given coccidiostats either through feed or water where there is a high risk of coccidiosis. Start it in the third week as a continuous prophylactic programme. Deworm the chicks with proper anthelmintics every 60 – 90 days to prevent intestinal worms. All of these measures should be done under the advice of a qualified veterinarian. Moreover, follow all the hygienic measures you have learnt in the previous chapter.

Management of layer chickens from the 9th week to the point of laying

This stage is known as the growing period and the chickens in this stage are called pullets or growers. The management of growers greatly determines the performance in the laying stage. Therefore, ensure good management of growers in aspects of housing, stocking rate, feeding, lighting, as well as parasite and disease management. Other aspects of grower management include culling and beak trimming.

Housing for layer growers

In large poultry farms, growers have to be transferred from the brooding house to the growing house at the age of 4 – 6 weeks. However, in small and medium poultry farms, the common practice is to use the same house for brooding and growing pullets. The average for birds on litter floors is 8 - 10 birds per square metre. The house should have good ventilation. A favourable temperature of about 24°C has also to be maintained. If layers are later to be reared in a battery cage system, pullets must be transferred to cages at 16 weeks of age.

During the growing period, litter should contain between 20 and 30% moisture. This moisture content is important for better feathering, improved feed use, normal growth, reduced ammonia level in the house and control of some diseases, for example, coccidiosis. The condition of the litter can be determined by holding it.
