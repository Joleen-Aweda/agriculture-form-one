Duck eggs can be incubated naturally or artificially. Commercial duck breeds cannot incubate their eggs because they are non-broody. For natural incubation, good broody chickens and ducks are chosen and used for hatching duck eggs. Duck eggs, except some few breeds take 28 days to hatch. On day 10 or 11, candling is usually done to check for development.

Parasite and disease management in ducks

Ducks are known to be hard against parasites and diseases. However, several parasites can affect them. Lice, mites, fleas, flukes and roundworms are examples of parasites that can infest ducks. Flukes are flatworms that can infect the liver, gallbladder, and intestine of ducks. The symptoms of fluke infection include weight loss, anaemia, and jaundice. Roundworms can infect the intestines of ducks causing weight loss, diarrhoea, and anaemia. Duck lice are small insects that feed on the feathers and skin of ducks causing irritation and potential secondary infections. Practical ways of managing parasites in ducks include the following:

  • (a) Practise good hygiene by washing hands thoroughly before and after handling ducks and keeping their food and water clean;
  • (b) Regularly inspect ducks for signs of parasites, such as weight loss, lethargy, and diarrhoea;
  • (c) Use approved pesticides, such as powders or sprays, to treat external parasites such as fleas, mites, and lice;
  • (d) Clean the duck houses and runs thoroughly to prevent re-infestation;
  • (e) Deworm ducks regularly to prevent intestinal worms; and
  • (f) Quarantine new birds to prevent the spread of external parasites to the entire flock.

Likewise, diseases such as duck viral enteritis, avian influenza, duck plague, and fowl cholera can affect ducks. Generally, diseases in ducks can be managed through the following ways:

  • (a) Vaccinating ducks against common viral diseases with the advice of a veterinarian or poultry experts.
  • (b) Providing ducks with clean and hygienic housing conditions.
  • (c) Ensuring balanced feeds and proper nutrition for ducks to prevent nutritional disorders.
  • (d) Regular monitoring of duck health and early detection of symptoms.
  • (e) Isolating sick ducks to prevent the spread of diseases.
