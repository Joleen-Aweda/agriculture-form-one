Choosing a site for onion production

We need to think about the type of soil, its pH level, altitude of the place, amount of rainfall and temperature requirement. Good conditions can make big and healthy onions. Onions can grow in many soil types, but they like loamy soil that drains well and has a good amount of organic matter. The soil pH should be between 5.5 and 6.8. The place should be at an altitude of 0 - 1900 m above sea level. A rainfall of 500 - 700 mm per year is good, but most onions are grown under irrigation. The best temperature for onions is between 15 and 25 degrees Celsius. Higher temperatures can make the onions form bulbs faster, but the bulbs get smaller. Lower temperatures can make the onions start to form flowers earlier instead of bulbs in the first growing season.

Preparation of seedbed for onion

Onions on a small scale can be grown in different seedbeds including improvised beds. These can be made from materials such as used containers, tyres, sacks, polythene bags and pots filled with soil or other growing media. For large-scale production, onions are grown in open fields or greenhouses.

To prepare seedbeds in an open field, follow these steps:

(i)

Dig the soil at least 25 - 30 cm deep. Then smoothen and level it to create a fine soil.

(ii)

Make seedbeds based on the season, type of irrigation and field condition.

(iii)

Add about 25 - 40 tons of manure or compost per hectare (equivalent to 20 kg/m²) during land preparation.

(iv)

Spread about 20 g/m² of inorganic fertiliser such as Di-Ammonia Phosphate (DAP) or Triple Sulphate Phosphate (TSP). For a small seedbed, measure its area. Then work out how much to use on that bed.

Remember, these steps are for open fields. If you are using improvised seedbeds, you may need to adjust the steps based on the materials you have. For example, you may need to add more soil if you are using containers or pots, or you may need to use different types of compost or manure. Always try to use what you have and what is available in your area.
