    In an intensive system, each duck requires a floor space of 3,710 - 4,650 cm². In battery cages, the floor space can be reduced to 1,350 cm² per duck. In a semi-intensive system, ducks need a floor space of 2,790 cm² in the night shelter and 929 - 1,395 cm² as an outside run. Layer ducks should be provided with laying nest boxes. A nest box measuring 30 cm wide, 45 cm deep, and 30 cm high is sufficient. One nest box should be provided for every 3 ducks.

Where ducks are reared in a semi-intensive system, the run can be provided with a pool. Apart from drinking, ducks need water for bathing and entertainment. Naturally, ducks enjoy swimming and splashing around in the water. They can even dive underwater to find food. It is recommended to wait until ducklings (young ducks) are at least 5 - 6 weeks old before introducing them to water. This allows ducklings to grow larger, develop their feathers, and handle changes in temperature better.

To manage a water pool in duck farming, it needs to provide sufficient water space and maintain water quality. Ensure the duck’s water pool remains clean and free from contaminants by regular cleaning and changing the water.

Feeding management of ducks

Ducks should be fed a balanced diet that includes carbohydrates, proteins, vitamins, and minerals. Ducks can be fed with locally available feeds or commercial feeds prepared by authorised distributors. Ducklings should be fed a starter diet from hatching to 4 weeks of age and then switched to a grower feed after 2 weeks. These feeds can be in the form of mash, crumble, or pellet. If special duck feeds are not available, standard poultry feeds can be used. Wet mash is commonly used and should be fed as a wet mash.

If ducklings are brooded artificially, they should be fed as soon as they are placed in the brooder. However, it is important not to feed ducks on poultry feed that contains a coccidiostat as it can be harmful to ducklings. Ducks can be switched to grower feed at 5 weeks of age, and this feed should be used until the ducks reach sexual maturity at 24 weeks of age.
