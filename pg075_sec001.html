Examples include parasitic wasps preying on whitefly and mealy bugs, ladybird beetle preying on aphids, praying mantis preying on the giant looper, weaver ants preying on white scales, chicken preying on stainers, chameleons preying on most insects, and cats preying on rats, mice and moles, wasps and poultry preying on bollworms. When a farm has conducive environment, it encourages these good creatures (predators) to live. In this way, the farm’s ecosystem helps to control the pests.

By using biological pest management, farmers help to keep the environment healthy and sustainable. However, it is important to note that biological pest management needs careful planning and management. The farmer needs to introduce/maintain the right good creatures and make sure they have the conditions they need to stay alive and thrive. Biological pest management controls pests while keeping the farm environment healthy and balanced. However, the process is a bit slow in some cases.

Chemical pest management

Chemical pest control is a method of controlling pests using chemicals, also known as pesticides. These chemicals come in different forms, such as powders, liquids, and gases. They can be sprayed, dusted, or fumigated onto soil and crop plants. Pesticides are grouped based on their formulation, the type of pest they target, and how they work. Some pesticides are designed to kill specific types of pests for example, insecticides kill insect pests, nematicides kill nematodes, rodenticides kill rodents, fungicides kill fungi, bactericides kill bacteria and herbicides kill weeds. Pesticides can also be either synthetic or bio-pesticides. It is recommended to use bio-pesticides (that is the pesticides coming from animal and plant by-products).

Key factors for pest control using pesticides:

When using pesticides to control pests, consider these factors:
(a)

Know the targeted pest: It is important to know what kind of pest you are dealing with. Different pests need different kinds of pesticides.

(b)

Choose the right pesticide: Not all pesticides work the same. Choose one that is known to work well against the targeted pest.

(c)

Rotating different pesticides: Sometimes pests get used to a certain pesticide in such a way that the pesticide becomes no longer effective on the targeted pest. In this case, you should try a different one.
