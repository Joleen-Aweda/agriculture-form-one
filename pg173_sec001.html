Preventive measures used to manage poultry parasites and diseases:

The preventive measures used in managing poultry parasites and diseases include vaccination, control of vectors, use of prophylactics, quarantine/isolation, sanitation, and proper nutrition. These preventive measures are elaborated as follows:

(a) Vaccination

Vaccination refers to the introduction of a vaccine into the animal body to protect from a specific disease. A vaccine is a biological product which is administered to the animal body to stimulate the production of antibodies against specific diseases caused by micro-organisms. There are various ways of administering vaccines to animals depending on the type of vaccine and manufacturer’s instruction. All vaccines should be administered under technical advice from extension workers or qualified veterinarians.

It is important to follow the manufacturers’ instructions on the storage and administration of the vaccines. Generally, vaccines should be stored at low temperatures of between 2°C and 8°C and they should not be exposed to direct sunlight. It is required to treat water before providing vaccines through drinking water. A teacher or livestock extension worker can assist with this. Birds should be thoroughly observed to ensure that every bird has taken the vaccine within the allowed time limit. Thereafter, drinkers should be thoroughly cleaned and refilled. It is important not to give a vaccine to sick animals or those taking the medication until they are fully recovered. At least 2 days should pass after their last dose. Moreover, it is not recommended to mix water containing a vaccine with vitamin supplements.

(b) Use of prophylactics

This involves giving healthy animals drugs at regular intervals to avoid infection. Chickens especially layers may be given coccidiostats either through feed or water where there is a high risk of coccidiosis. This disease causes severe losses in young and growing chickens. Coccidiostat is a substance that can be given to birds to stop or slow down the growth of the protozoa parasites that cause coccidiosis disease. The administration of coccidiostat should commence in the third week as a continuous prophylactic programme. However, to minimise the risk of resistance to a particular coccidiostat, it is common to use different coccidiostats in each production cycle or even within the same cycle. Deworming with proper anthelmintics every 60 – 90 days is another form of prophylaxis against intestinal worms. All prophylactic measures should be done under the supervision or advice of a qualified veterinarian.
