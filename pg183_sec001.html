Pick up a handful of litter and squeeze it tightly then open the hand. If the condition of the litter is alright, crevices will form in the compressed material. It will not form a cohesive ball or fall away in a pile. If possible, special devices can be used for determining the moisture percentage in the litter. Pullets which are later intended to be raised in deep litter-laying houses have to be provided with properly placed perches.

Feeding of layer growers

At the growing stage, the feed is changed from chick starter to grower mash until the chickens start laying by 3%. Grower mash is poor in energy and low in protein compared to chick mash. This aims to control the growth rate of the growers. Pullets are not encouraged to grow and mature before the intended age. Unless a management guide is provided by the breeder, restricted feeding of pullets should be practised (refer to Table 11.4). The daily feed allowance is provided twice a day to control selection and encourage feed intake. This should be done at the same time every day. They should be given 60% of their feed in the morning and 40% in the afternoon. Sick birds and those which have just undergone vaccination or beak trimming should be fed without restriction until they feel better.
