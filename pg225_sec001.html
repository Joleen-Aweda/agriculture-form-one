Two weeks before laying, the daily ration should be increased to 220 grams per day if feeding dry mash, and to 330 grams per day if feeding wet mash. Ducklings should be provided with grit regularly, especially if they are not foraging for their food. Feeding grits allows ducklings to regulate their intake and it ensures proper digestion.

During the laying period (25 - 66 weeks), the feed intake of ducks should be increased to 325 grams per day if using dry mash, and to 485 grams per day if using wet mash. The 50% egg-laying stage is expected to be reached at around 25 - 26 weeks of age. From this stage onwards, ducks should be given a minimum of 0.5 kg of feed per duck per day. Any type of feeder that can be used for feeding chickens can also be used for feeding ducks. Layer ducks reared in an intensive system are fed twice a day, in the morning and evening. They should be fed an amount that can be finished in about 10 - 15 minutes. Layer ducks can be fed with either mash or pellets, but wet mash is preferred. For feeding, one long feeder can be used by 20 ducks and one round feeder can be used by 10 - 15 ducks. In addition to proper nutrition, providing ducks with grit is important for their digestion and overall health.

Clean water is essential for ducks, and it should be provided in standing troughs with wire mesh to prevent ducks from wetting their bodies. Drinkers should be arranged so that ducks can submerge their bills in the water and clear their nostrils by squirting water through them. Cleanliness of feeders and waterers should be maintained to ensure flock health.

Feeding of ducks in integrated farming:

Ducks fit well in integrated farming systems such as duck-cum-fish farming. In these systems, ducks get most of their feed from the pond, which includes aquatic weeds, insects, larvae, and earthworms. At the same time, fish can use waste from duck droppings and feed particles that fall into the pond. When ducks are allowed to roam freely over the pond surface, their droppings fertilise the pond, stimulating the growth of natural foods for both fish and ducks.

Mating and incubation management in ducks

Male ducks, known as drakes, can serve six female ducks. However, if the farmer needs more fertile eggs, then one drake should serve only four ducks. Ducks usually start laying eggs when they are around 5 - 6 months old. The highest egg production occurs about 5 - 6 weeks after they start laying. To induce high egg production, ducks need a lighting period of 14 hours.
