The following are the steps to grow onion seedlings in the nursery:

(i)

Drill lines 15 – 20 cm apart and 1- 1.5 cm deep across the nursery bed.

(ii)

Put seeds in the lines thinly. Cover them lightly with a fine soil.

(iii)

Spread light dry grass mulch on top.

(iv)

Water lightly to avoid too much moisture. Too much water can make it easier for the ‘damping off’ disease to develop.

(v)

The seeds start to grow after 5 - 10 days. After this time, remove the mulch. If you delay this step, the seedlings will become too thin and slender.

(vi)

Water when needed to keep the soil moist for the seedlings.

(vii)

Put up shade that is about 1 m high to protect seedlings from strong sunlight.

(viii)

Check the plants often and deal with any pest or disease problems. Checking and controlling the ‘damping-off’ disease and cutworms are very important at this stage.

Transplanting onions

Transplanting should be in the cool part of the day. Plant the seedlings after 35 to 50 days or when they have 3 - 5 leaves and the stem is like a pencil thickness.

For easy field management, divide the field into plots and water them before transplanting. Before transplanting, water the nursery full 2 hours before to make it easier to uproot the seedlings. When planting, put the seedlings 15 - 20 cm from one row to another and 7 - 10 cm from one seedling to another in the rows. Plant them about 2 - 3 cm deep. After transplanting, water the seedlings moret often until they are well established.

The way you plant can change based on the type of onion and what people want in the market. Also, you can use tools such as dibblers to help plant at the right distance. Figure 7.2 shows examples of transplanting dibblers.
