Availability of resources for vegetable production

Starting a vegetable business depends on several resources. Those resources can vary based on your location and the specific vegetables you want to grow. It is important to carefully consider each resource before starting your vegetable business. These include:

  • (a)

    Land: You need to know where you will grow your vegetables, how much land you have, and the type of vegetable to grow.

  • (b)

    Climate: You need to know whether the intended horticultural crop is suitable to the climate of the area.

  • (c)

    Water: You need to know where you will get water from and how much it will cost. You also need to know if you can use this water for your vegetables.

  • (d)

    Structures: You need to know the needed structures such as buildings and fences. You also need to know how much they cost to keep them in good condition.

  • (e)

    Tools/equipment: You need to know the tools and equipment you have to use in your vegetable business. You also need to know if you need to rent or borrow tools/equipment and estimate cost.

  • (f)

    Money for capital: You need to know how much money you have to start your business. You also need to identify the source of fund for establishing and running the business.

  • (g)

    Management skills: You need to know how to keep records, manage people, and prepare your budget.

  • (h)

    Labour: You need to know how many people are required for your vegetable business. You also need to know if you can use family or hired labour.

  • (i)

    Market and marketing: You need to know where your customers are and how you will take your vegetables to them or whether they will take the vegetables directly from your farm. You also need to decide on a reasonable and profitable selling price.

Matching vegetable enterprises with the available resources

Plan your vegetable enterprises with available resources. These resources include water, land, money and equipment you have. Compare your resources with the needs of each crop. If a crop needs a lot of sun and you have a lot of it, that is good. If a crop needs rich soil and you have poor soil, you may need to apply fertilisers. If you are not sure, ask a local farmer or an expert. They can give you good advice based on their expertise and experience. Use resources such as books, the internet, and local libraries to learn more. Several websites and books can help you to understand which crops are compatible with each other and with your resources.
