Harvesting and postharvesting practices in vegetable production

Harvesting is a process of removing the entire plant or economic parts after maturity for consumption, marketing, storage or processing. Harvested part(s) or the whole plant to be harvested must have attained the right stage of maturity. Therefore, it is important to have knowledge of maturity indices for different types of vegetable crops as over-mature and premature harvest affects negatively the quality of the produce and shelf life. The method of harvesting depends on the type of vegetable crop. Ensure proper harvest without causing any damage to the plant or produce.

Postharvesting practices in vegetable crop production

Postharvesting practices in vegetable crops refer to the processes that follow after vegetables are harvested. These practices intend to make the vegetables ready for eating, easy to handle and last longer. These practices depend on what kind of vegetables we have, how we want to use them, and when we want to use them. For most vegetables, these practices might include cooling, cleaning, sorting and grading, packaging, storing, or transporting them. We can also use special treatments like refrigeration, heat treatment, and modified atmosphere packaging to keep our vegetables fresh and good to eat for a longer time.
