Duck meat handling and value addition:

Ensure that ducks are slaughtered and processed in a clean and hygienic environment to maintain meat safety and quality. Follow the following procedures:

(i)

Fast the ducks for 12 - 24 hours before slaughter to empty their digestive system.

(ii)

Pluck the ducks to remove feathers after slaughter.

(iii)

After plucking, dress it. Take out the internal organs and the legs. Then, wash the duck well. Leave it as a whole carcass or cut it into specific cuts depending on your market preference. After it cools down, it is ready to be packaged.

(iv)

Package it for sale at the butchery. Wrap each whole duck carcasses or cuts in thin-layer polythene or any other materials that are not corrosive to meat. Label the package properly to provide information about the product, including its origin, production method, and handling instructions.

(v)

Store the packaged duck carcass in the freezer ready for sale.

Marketing of live ducks and duck meat:

The marketing of live ducks and duck meat is subject to regulations governed by various regulatory bodies to ensure food safety and quality. You may not know all these, but livestock extension workers or poultry experts can advise you about the regulations. The marketing process may involve various stakeholders such as farmers, processors, distributors, and retailers. Cold storage and transportation facilities should be used to maintain the quality and freshness of the duck meat during marketing. Where live ducks are sold, they have to be handled and transported properly.

Record keeping and Management in ducks

Maintaining accurate and detailed records is important for tracking the performance, health, and production of ducks. Records should include information on mating/breeding, feeding, health treatments, egg production, and sales. Digital tools can be used for record-keeping processes and to improve data management.
