Volatilization:

Some nutrients in the soil can be lost through volatilization.

Volatilization is when nutrients in the soil turn into gas and rise into the air instead of staying in the soil to feed the plants.

The most common nutrients lost through volatilization, especially in hot weather, are nitrogen, sulphur, and to a lesser extent, phosphorus.

To prevent this, apply nitrogenous and sulphur fertilisers by incorporating them into the soil rather than leaving them on the surface.

Also, avoid applying fertilisers during hot, dry conditions when volatilization is more likely to occur.

The use of organic fertilisers in maintaining soil fertility

Organic fertilisers are nutrient-carrying materials derived from plants and animal materials also known as manures or natural manures. These materials play a great role in improving soil fertility. They add nutrients such as nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium to the soil, which are needed by plants to grow.

There are different types of organic fertilisers including manures. Examples of manures are farmyard manure, compost, and green manure. Farmyard manure is made from the waste of farm animals, for example, faeces and urine, along with beddings and leftover feed. It is important to store it properly to avoid losing nutrients. It can be used in fields after being well-decomposed. Compost is made from organic materials or waste. It is made by piling up farm residues and covering them with animal waste. Compost is ready to use when it turns dark brown or black, soft and easy to spread, and the pile gets smaller. Green manure is made by growing a crop on a piece of land and then incorporating it by ploughing it into the soil at the flowering stage.
