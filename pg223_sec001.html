Management of ducks

Looking after ducks is like looking after chickens. But, there are some differences. We need to think about housing and feeding them. We also need to think about managing their mating and incubation as well as keeping them healthy. Each of the following subsections will help you to take care of your ducks properly.

Housing management for ducks

Ducks are waterfowl. They need shelter and they don’t like wind or excessive sunlight. When resting, they require a dry and sheltered nesting place. Providing ducks with housing is important to protect them from harsh weather conditions, predators and to facilitate egg collection. Here are some key points to consider when providing housing for ducks:

  • (a) Ducks can be housed in a conventional house or a simple shed made from locally available resources.
  • (b) The housing should protect ducks from wind, rain, and sun. It should have adequate room ventilation and a dry bed.
  • (c) Normally, ducks lay eggs at night or in the morning usually between 6:00 a.m to noon, so housing prevents losses from predators and it makes egg collection easier.
  • (d) If possible, using a free-range system where ducks are allowed to disperse or keeping only one flock can save on feeding and penning costs.
  • (e) The recommended space for free-ranging ducks is approximately 0.5 hectares per 100 ducks.
  • (f) The size of the entrance should be large enough for the biggest duck to enter the housing smoothly.
  • (g) If ducks are allowed to range freely, it is important to rotate enclosed ranges, offer shade and shelter from bad weather, and avoid confining them for too long during daylight hours.
