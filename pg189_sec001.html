Feeding the mature layer chickens

Feed the birds twice a day using appropriate feed and feeders placed at a distance to allow free movement in case of a deep litter system. A laying bird requires to be fed an appropriate amount of layers mash per day depending on the heaviness of the breed. Light breeds (110 – 115 g/birds/day), medium breeds (120 g/bird/day), and heavy breeds (130 – 140 g/bird/day). Layers should also be provided with cool and clean water throughout the day (ad libitum). Check if water consumption is normal because if there is any problem with the flock, consumption is reduced.

Culling in the laying period

During this period, there are two kinds of culling. These are the culling of non-laying birds after the laying peak and the culling of the laying flock after the economic laying is completed.

Culling of non-laying birds after the laying peak:

Culling of non-laying birds involves identifying and removing non-laying birds from the flock. Properly reared layers reach the laying peak one month after laying begins, however, it may take up to 2 months when birds are not well maintained. Peak can be around 24 weeks of age when laying is 90% or more. There are several characteristics which can be used to identify non-laying hens from the laying ones. The identification exercise for this purpose has to be carried out by an experienced poultry expert. It is also advisable to isolate the identified non-laying birds for some days. This helps the poultry farmer to check if those birds are truly not laying eggs. The culling exercise can disturb the flock thereby lowering egg production. To minimise this negative effect on the flock, culling exercises should be practised at night.

Culling of the laying flock after the economic laying is completed:

From the 24th week of age, well-managed layers are expected to lay productively for 12 – 14 months. After this period, laying will have dropped to 50%. At this stage, it is no longer economical to continue keeping the birds. Unless the egg prices are very high or you feed a very cheap feed, you should cull them and replace the flock. The economic laying is 60% or above. The culling processes may start from the 10th month of the laying period. This will allow the elimination of non-productive chickens and reducing the management of non-laying hens.
