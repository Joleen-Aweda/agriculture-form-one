• (iv)

    If you are growing seedlings using drip irrigation and they are indeterminate varieties, transplant them 150 cm apart in the row (between the rows) and 50 cm apart in the same row (between the plants);

  • (v)

    After moving the seedlings, water them immediately. The amount and frequency of watering depend on the moisture of the soil;

  • (vi)

    To keep the appropriate number of plants in the field, replace dead plants (gap filling) 10 days after transplanting them.

It is better to transplant seedlings prepared in a ground nursery early in the morning, evening, or on a cloudy day. This helps to reduce the shock when they are transplanted. Seedlings prepared on trays can be transplanted at any time.

Management of water for tomato plants

Tomatoes need sufficient water to grow properly. The more they grow, the more water they need. This is especially true after they are transplanted from their pot to the garden, and when they are flowering and making fruits. The best times to irrigate tomatoes depend on the season. In the dry season, plants can be irrigated 3 to 4 times a week. In wet season, you can water them less often. You can irrigate tomatoes in different ways including furrow and drip irrigation. You can also water just the base of the plant with a bucket or hose pipe. Remember, it is important to water tomatoes, but too much water can also harm them. Therefore, it is advisable to irrigate tomatoes when the soil is dry.

Management of nutrients for tomato plants

There are two main sources of nutrients you can use to help tomatoes grow well. The first is organic manures, for example, farmyard and compost manure. The second is chemical or industrial fertilisers. If you only use organic manure, you should apply about 12 - 25 tons per hectare per cropping season. For high-yield tomato varieties, you should apply 100 - 150 kg/ha of nitrogen, 65 - 110 kg/ha of phosphate and 160 - 240 kg/ha of potash.

Industrial fertilisers should be applied at a range between 40 - 120 kg/ha of nitrogen, 30 – 90 kg/ha of phosphate, and 30 - 90 kg/ha of potash. When applying industrial fertilisers, be careful not to let them touch the young or wet plants, as this can burn them. The way a tomato plant takes up nutrients depends on the type of fertiliser and how it is applied. You can apply it directly to the soil, about 10 cm away from the stem and 5 cm deep, using either the ring or dibbling method.
