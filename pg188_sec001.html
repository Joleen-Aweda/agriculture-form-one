8. What are the key considerations for lighting in layer growers, and why is it important to gradually increase the light exposure for pullets?

Management of layer chickens from the point of laying to final culling

Birds are placed in a laying house just before the onset of the laying period. Laying starts at the age of 17 - 20 weeks. Laying lasts for 12 to 14 months for optimal production. During this period, the important management aspects that should be observed are feeding, provision of water, ventilation, parasites and disease management. It also involves adhering to hygiene and biosecurity measures as discussed in previous chapters. In the case of the cage system, the birds are introduced to the cages a few days before the onset of the laying period. This is to make them familiarize themselves with the new confinement.

The housing of mature layer chickens

Continue following the housing management aspects you have already learnt especially maintenance of cleanliness. Also, provide the chickens with laying nests, proper living space as well as lighting and ventilation.

Placing the laying boxes:

Laying boxes are also termed as laying nests. They are to be placed in the laying house well before the hens are brought in, preferably in 16th week. The nest boxes should be provided with plenty of good clean litter such as wood shavings or other nesting materials to avoid cracking of eggs. They should also be placed at relatively lower heights compared to perches. This will discourage chickens from using them to roost and also preventing chickens and eggs from becoming dirty.

The stocking rate for mature layer chickens:

At this stage, the stocking rate should be maintained at 4 - 5 birds per m² in a deep litter system. In the case of battery cages, it is better not to keep more than 12 birds per cage. Typically, you can keep 2 – 10 hens per cage, which can be equated to 18 hens per m².

Lighting and ventilation for mature layer chickens:

For optimal laying, layers should be provided with lighting for 16 hours daily up to the final culling. Therefore, layers should be provided with artificial light for some hours. The hours of artificial light should be determined by the length of daylight in a given area. Likewise, the houses for laying birds should be well-ventilated to allow air circulation.
