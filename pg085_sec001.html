Management of nutrients to amaranth

Amaranth is a crop that needs a lot of nutrients. You can use organic or synthetic fertilisers based on the plant’s needs. If you want to harvest the whole amaranth plant, it is advisable to improve the soil’s fertility before planting. This can be done by adding about 20 - 25 tons of well-decomposed manure per hectare (2 - 2.5 kg per m²) at the ploughing stage. You can also use other chemical fertilisers such as Nitrogen, Phosphorous, and Potash (NPK) at planting. The recommended ratio is 10:10:20. Also, spraying the crop with 1% urea or diluted cow urine after every harvest can help the plants to grow more and yield more.

Management of water to amaranth

Amaranth requires a constant amount of water, especially during germination and at the early growth stage. You should water the plants regularly, but not too much because too much water can lead to a disease called damping off. It is better to water the plants when the air temperature is lower, like in the morning or evening. There are several ways to water amaranth plants, including watering through furrows, overhead watering or basin irrigation and using watering can (especially for small scale or home garden).

Weed management in amaranth

Practices to minimise weeds and weed seeds must be employed before sowing. This may involve deep ploughing to kill both annual and perennial weeds. The use of non-selective systematic herbicides may be applied to kill weeds such as nut grass before ploughing. Irrigation to a ploughed land before sowing will promote the growing of new weeds which will have to be removed before sowing. Other practises, for example, hand pulling and weeding, can be applied after the crop has been established.

Pest and disease management

Amaranth plants can be harvested shortly after planting. This means that the problems with pests must be dealt with early, even before sowing.
