These chickens can be raised in normal environments just like indigenous chickens. These chickens can be reared in all systems of poultry farming, that is, intensive, semi-intensive, and extensive or free-range. Examples of dual-purpose hybrid chickens are as follow:

(a) Sasso: This is a hybrid chicken that is a crossbreed between indigenous hens and a superior meat cock. Sasso can be reared for meat and egg production. If reared for meat and given fattening feed, they can weigh more than 2.5 kg within 70 - 90 days. If reared for eggs, they start laying eggs at the age of 20 weeks and can lay up to 240 eggs throughout their laying period.

(b) Kuroiler: These are hybrid chickens bred after crossing indigenous hens and a superior egg-laying cock. Kuroiler is also reared for meat and eggs. If reared for eggs, these chickens can lay 190 – 250 eggs within 30 months. Kuroiler starts laying eggs at just 5 months, unlike the local chickens which start laying eggs at 6 months. Kuroiler lays for 2 years if well taken care of. Kuroilers are also good for meat because the cocks can reach a weight of up to 5 kg and the hens can reach between 3.5 to 4 kg.

(c) Tanbro: These chickens grow quickly and have large bodies compared to local chickens, making them very good for meat. If reared for eggs, these chickens can lay 180 - 220 eggs within 81 weeks. Tanbro starts laying eggs at 19 - 21 weeks of age. Tanbro is also good for meat because the pullets can reach a weight of up to 1.5 kg within 7 weeks and 2 kg within 9 weeks and the cocks can reach 4 kg if well cared for. These have good disease resistance compared to meat or egg chickens.

In Tanzania, there are several kinds of dual-purpose hybrid chickens. Whichever you choose to raise depends on what you want to achieve and what the people in your area need. To choose the right hybrid, you should think about where you live and what type of chicken people like. It is advisable to ask livestock extension workers and/or experienced poultry farmers for opinions.

Looking after dual-purpose hybrid chickens is not much different from taking care of other types of chickens. The main difference is in feeding them. This depends on whether you are raising them for their meat or eggs. The feeding schedule when raised for meat is as follows:

(a) From day 1 to 18, or until they weigh 400 grams, give them broiler starter crumble. If they reach 400 grams before day 18, start feeding broiler grower pellets.
