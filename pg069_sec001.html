Management of soil water for vegetable production

Soil water helps plants to grow and stay healthy. Plants lose water through transpiration. This is when water turns into water vapour and disappears from the plants’ leaves. Water also leaves the soil through a process called evaporation. The loss of water from both the soil and plants is called evapotranspiration. If we don’t add water to the soil, plants can die.

Different types of plants need different amounts of water. Some plants can tolerate high amount of water, and others cannot. Similarly, the amount of water needed depends on the stage of growth and the weather situation. We can check how much water is in the soil by feeling and looking at it. This can be done by squeezing a soil sample and seeing how it feels and looks. If plants have too little water, they start wilting and leaves turn brown. If they have too much water, their leaves can become soft and limp, also can start wilting due to root suffocation and disease infestation.

Certain stages of plant growth are more sensitive to water shortage. If we don’t give plants enough water during these growth stages yield and quality of produce are severely affected. We can conserve moisture in the soil through the application of compost or manure over the soil, placing mulch on the soil and practising crop rotation. This helps to reduce the frequency of watering or irrigating the crop. The watering of the plants is called irrigation. There are different methods to irrigate vegetable crops.
