(c) Quarantine and Isolation

During the outbreaks of highly infectious diseases such as avian influenza, the movements of birds and their by-products are restricted to avoid further spread of diseases to other areas. Likewise, sick birds should be isolated from the healthy ones to avoid the spread of the disease.

(d) Strict following of hygienic measures

This involves practices that prevent the spread of disease-causing agents. The following are hygienic measures which can be practised to prevent parasites and diseases in poultry:

(i)

Practise an all-in all-out system to reduce the risk of contamination. This system requires removing all birds from a room or building then cleaning and disinfecting it before bringing in a new batch.

(ii)

Prevent people from entering the brooder house or poultry premises without permission.

(iii)

Provide a room at the entrance that has a special dustbin to collect waste, a place to wash hands with soap and running water, and a place to change clothes and shoes. Footbaths should also be used and managed properly.

(iv)

Everyone must change shoes when entering or leaving a poultry house.

(v)

Clean the drinkers and disinfect them at least once a week.

(vi)

Remove dust on all surfaces.

(vii)

Control vermin, flies and other external parasites.

(e) Proper feeding

Proper feeding of animals with adequate feeds in terms of quality and quantity helps to prevent nutritional deficiency diseases, for example, avitaminosis. It also prevents animals from getting weak and generally poor growth and production.

(f) Proper housing design

Good design of chicken house provides the best internal conditions, for example, good ventilation. This helps to reduce the chances of occurrence of some parasites and diseases hence keeping the birds healthy.
