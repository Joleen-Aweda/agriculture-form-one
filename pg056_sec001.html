Growing considerations

The following can also be put into consideration when planning for vegetable crop production:

(a)

Crop rotation: If you plan to grow different crops in rotations, you should know how they affect each other. Some crops can help or harm the growth of others.

(b)

Pests and diseases: Learn about common pests that can harm your crop, for example, insects, birds, wild or domestic animals, weeds and diseases and how to manage them.

(c)

Harvesting: You need to know when and how to harvest your crops.

(d)

Risks: Every crop comes with its own risks. Identify them and think before how to reduce them.

By considering all these things, you can grow your vegetable crops successfully.

Compatibility among farm enterprises

When you want to grow more than one type of crop, you have to think about how they fit together. For instance, you might have enough people to help with one crop. But, if you choose another crop that also needs a lot of labour, you might not have enough to help. It is important to do all activities timely and appropriately. In this case, you will be required to prepare a chart showing labour requirements at an appropriate time for each crop. When you want to farm both animals and vegetables, think about how they can be together. This is because some animals and crops don’t mix well. For example, free-range chickens can destroy some vegetables such as amaranth. However, they won’t cause any trouble with the production of cassava. Fencing can help to separate the two enterprises.

Possible vegetable enterprises

There are different ways to get involved in the vegetable industry. The following are some ideas:

(a)

Production of traditional vegetables: This involves growing vegetables such as nightshade, pumpkin leaves, amaranth, and sweet potato leaves.

(b)

Organic vegetable production enterprises: This involves of growing vegetables using non-synthetic/industrial or chemical fertilisers and pesticides.

(c)

Conventional vegetable production enterprises: Growing vegetables that involves using synthetic inputs such as fertilisers and pesticides.

(d)

Retail marketing of vegetables: This involves selling vegetables in small portions.
