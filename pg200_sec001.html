Feeding of broiler chickens

Broilers can be fed by using one of these two programmes:

(a)

Three feed types feeding schedule: This schedule involves feeding the birds with three types of feeds at different stages. These are broiler starter crumbles or mash, broiler grower pellets or mash and broiler finisher pellets or mash. These feeds have to be fed as follow:

(i)
Broiler starter crumble/mash from day one up to day 14 (2 weeks).
(ii)
Broiler grower pellet/mash from week three to week four.
(iii)
Broiler finisher pellet/mash from week 5 to marketing weight.
(b)

Two feed types feeding schedule: This schedule involves feeding the birds with two types of feeds. These are broiler starter crumbles and broiler finisher pellets/mash. These feeds have to be fed as follow:

(i)
Broiler starter crumble from day one up to day 21, and
(ii)
Finisher pellets from day 22 up to market point.

Such feeds may be either commercial feeds from the feed companies or homemade feeds using local ingredients/materials available by using local mixing methods to minimise feed costs. But, make sure you get assistance from a feed expert to get a good formula for the feed you wish to formulate. Provide vitamins and minerals to the chickens to avoid nutritional disorders.

Each feeding schedule has its advantages and challenges. The first schedule (starter, grower, and finisher type of feed) ensures a balanced growth and development of the chickens, but it can be more expensive. The second schedule (starter and finisher without grower type of feed) can be less expensive, but it might not provide the same level of nutrients for the chickens to grow and develop properly.

Stocking rate for broiler chickens

Birds should be provided with the least amount of floor space necessary to the greatest return on investment. The larger birds require more floor space than the smaller ones. The optimum stocking rates vary with the size of the bird to be marketed. For example, if the farmer is to sell small-sized birds of 1.4 kg in weight, there is a possibility of raising more birds in a unit area than raising large birds of 2.3 Kg.
