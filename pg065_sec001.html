(b)

Crop removal through continuous cropping and grazing:

When crops are continuously grown on the same land, it can deplete the soil of nutrients. This is called soil degradation. Grazing can also deplete the soil’s nutrients, especially if the animals are overgrazed. To address this, we can practice crop rotation or use rotational grazing. This helps to maintain a balance of nutrients in the soil. The lost nutrients should also be replaced by applying organic manure or inorganic fertilisers to the soil.

(c)

Using acid or alkali-forming fertilisers for a long time:

Using acid or alkali-forming fertilisers for a long time can change soil pH. Acidic fertilisers can lower soil pH. This can affect plant growth. Alkaline fertilisers can raise soil pH. This also can affect plant growth. Soil pH changes can affect soil micro-organisms and available nutrients. To avoid a pH decrease, don’t use too much acidic fertilisers. To avoid a pH increase, don’t use too much alkaline fertilisers. Always check the soil’s pH before adding fertilisers. You can test the soil at home with a pH meter. You can also seek advice for the selection of fertilisers from a crop extension worker. This will help to keep the soil’s pH balanced for plant growth.

(d)

Improper weed control methods:

Weeds are plants that grow where they are not needed. When we pull them out, we lose the nutrients they have taken from the soil. It is better to let them decompose into humus on the field. But, where weeds cause problems, we should remove them. To enhance weed control together with the management of soil fertility, we can do the following:

(i)

Composting:

Weeds that are removed can be composted, which means they are left to decompose naturally. This process turns them into a nutrient-rich compost that can be used to enrich the soil.

(ii)

Crop rotation:

This involves growing different types of crops in the same piece of land by alternation (refer to Figure 2.8). This method controls weeds which only grow well when in association with certain crops, for example, parasitic weeds. In addition, crop rotation helps to keep nutrients in the soil at a good level.
