Handling, value addition and marketing of duck eggs

This sub-section provides a brief overview of duck egg collection, handling, processing, storage, and marketing. For more detailed information and specific regulations, it is important to seek more information and advice from poultry extension workers or experts.

Duck egg collection:

Collect duck eggs at least twice a day (early morning and late afternoon) to ensure freshness. Handle them carefully to avoid cracking or damaging the shells. Use clean containers to collect the eggs.

Duck egg handling:

Handle the eggs with clean hands to prevent contamination. Clean the eggs by gently wiping off any dirt. Do not wash the eggs as it can remove the protective coating.

Duck egg grading and packaging:

Grade the duck eggs based on their size and quality. Use appropriate packaging materials to protect the eggs during transportation. Label the packages with relevant information for wholesalers, retailers, and consumers. Follow hygiene and biosecurity practices to ensure that the eggs are not contaminated during processing.

Duck egg storage:

Use clean containers to store the eggs in a cool and dry place to maintain freshness. Also, keep them away from contaminants and predators. Follow storage guidelines to ensure that the eggs are safe for consumption. Regularly check for any damaged or spoiled eggs and discard them.

Duck egg marketing:

Assess market requirements and choose appropriate marketing channels. Work with collectors, assembly merchants, wholesalers, and retailers in the marketing process. Consider factors such as market demand, intermediaries available, and costs involved. Follow regulations and guidelines for packaging, labelling, and transportation of duck eggs.
