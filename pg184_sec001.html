Follow-up of pullet growth progress should be done. The flock’s average progress in weight gain must be used in making feeding decisions. If on weekly weighing the birds are too heavy then the daily feed allowance should be reduced. If pullets are too light, the daily allowance should be increased. Pullets should be provided with vitamin supplements after weekly weight measurements to help them overcome stress. Changing of type of feed must be done by introducing new feed slowly while reducing the former. For example, changing from chick starter to grower mash can be done as follows:

  • Day 1: 75% Chick starter mash & 25% Grower mash
  • Day 2: 50% Chick starter mash & 50% Grower mash
  • Day 3: 25% Chick starter mash & 75% Grower mash
  • Day 4: 0% Chick starter mash & 100% Grower mash

Pullets need to be provided with enough space to reach the feed. Where restricted feeding is practised, enough feeders must be provided. This helps all birds to eat at the same time when the feed is provided. When feeding space is inadequate, the aggressive birds would eat more feed than the docile ones. The height of the feeder should be adjusted in such a way that the bottom of the trough is at an even level with the backs of the birds. When the level is lower, birds peck out more feed which is wastage.

In addition, pullets should be provided with water all the time. An adequate number of drinkers should be provided and arranged properly in the pullet house. A 10-litre round plastic drinker can serve up to 50 pullets. The water requirement for pullets is twice as much as feed intake, and even more for temperatures above 21.5°C. Drinkers should accordingly be adjusted as you have already learnt (in chick management). Drinkers should thoroughly be cleaned and the water refilled frequently.

Lighting in layer growers

Growers are raised such that they don’t get extra light until 13 weeks of age. This is done to control or set sexual maturity to the intended period. After 13 weeks, light is gradually increased to 16 hours per day (refer to Table 11.5). This encourages sexual maturity and sets the point of laying at 17 - 18 weeks. A 3-watt/m2 light brightness is enough for this purpose. Too bright light is not recommended. You have to determine daylight for your local area to use artificial light to complete the required light schedule, if necessary. Light (either natural or artificial), affects the pituitary gland at the base of the brain, and the stimulation causes mature pullets to begin the production of eggs.
